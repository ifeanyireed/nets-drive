Corridor Alert

Federal Ministry of Works Accelerates Sagamu-Ikorodu & Lagos-Abeokuta Arterial Upgrades as Manufacturers Report Morning Shift Delays

The Federal Ministry of Works has intensified pavement reconstruction across the Sagamu-Ikorodu and Sango-Ota industrial arteries to expand multi-axle freight capacity. While long-term logistics will improve, ongoing construction diversions and rainy-season surface washouts have lengthened unmanaged worker commute times by an average of 38%, triggering severe 30- to 55-minute morning shift handover delays across FMCG and food processing plants.

► WHAT IT MEANS FOR PLANT OPERATORS

When machine operators arrive 35 minutes late, automated lines cannot start. Exhausted night-shift crews must be held on expensive overtime, captive gas turbines burn baseload fuel with zero product throughput, and steam pressure is wasted. Manufacturing operations relying on cash transport allowances are bearing the full cost of highway construction detours, whereas plants operating dedicated cluster shuttles bypass primary chokepoints via secondary arterial routes.

Industrial Operations Round-Up

Energy & Power

Commercial Gas Tariff Indexing Increases Hourly Cost of Captive Power Idling

With natural gas commodity pricing directly indexed to industrial power generation, operating a 3 MW captive gas turbine without product load now costs over ₦750,000 per hour. Every minute a packaging line waits for late workers is significantly more expensive than twelve months ago.

Source: BusinessDay Nigeria / Energy
Enforcement

Ogun State TRACE Enforces Strict Morning Lane Discipline on Sango-Ota Axis

The Ogun State Traffic Compliance and Enforcement Corps (TRACE) has deployed dedicated task forces to arrest informal Danfos driving against one-way traffic. Factories relying on public buses face mounting impoundment delays, reinforcing the need for compliant, vetted corporate shuttles.

Source: TRACE Ogun / Vanguard
Workforce Benchmark

MAN Survey: 22% Productivity Gap Between Dedicated Shuttles and Cash Allowances

A study of 120 processing and manufacturing plants revealed that facilities running dedicated, employer-managed staff bus fleets achieved an average 98.4% on-time shift handover, compared to just 76.2% for facilities relying on worker cash allowances.

Source: Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN)
