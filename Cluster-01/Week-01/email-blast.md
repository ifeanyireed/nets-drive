### Cover Story: Infrastructure & Industrial Transit
**HEADLINE: Federal Ministry of Works Accelerates Sagamu-Ikorodu and Lagos-Abeokuta Expressway Corridor Rehabilitation as Industrial Bottlenecks Mount**

*Photo: Heavy industrial haulage and worker commuter traffic navigating chokepoints along the Sagamu-Ikorodu industrial corridor. Source: Federal Ministry of Works / Punch Nigeria.*

The Federal Ministry of Works has stepped up arterial pavement reinforcement and bridge expansion projects along critical manufacturing belts in Ogun and Lagos States, notably the Sagamu-Ikorodu industrial artery and the Sango-Ota to Abeokuta corridor. While the long-term infrastructure investment aims to expand multi-axle freight capacity, ongoing construction detours and rainy season surface washouts have intensified early-morning transit gridlock for factory shift workers.

According to the Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN), plant operators in Agbara, Sango-Ota, and Sagamu report that unmanaged commercial commuter transit times have lengthened by 38% since August. Workers relying on informal Danfo buses and tricycles face average corridor delays of 30 to 55 minutes during the critical 5:00 AM – 6:30 AM morning shift handover window.

> **► WHAT IT MEANS FOR PLANT MANAGERS & OPERATIONS DIRECTORS:**
> For continuous manufacturing lines (FMCG, food packaging, plastics, and pharmaceutical packaging), an unmanaged workforce commute creates direct operational instability. When machine operators arrive late, high-speed automated packaging machinery sits cold, captive gas power turbines burn unrecovered baseload fuel, and exhausted night shifts must be held on expensive overtime. Plants that rely on cash transport allowances are bearing the full financial brunt of construction detours, whereas operators with dedicated, GPS-tracked cluster shuttles bypass primary chokepoints via pre-mapped industrial arterial routes.
