Corridor Intelligence

Federal Ministry of Works & TRACE Implement New Arterial Diversions at Sagamu Interchange as Morning Handover Delays Mount

The Federal Ministry of Works and the Ogun State Traffic Compliance and Enforcement Corps (TRACE) have initiated lane adjustments and surface reinforcement works across key industrial choke points near the Sagamu Interchange and the Ogijo-Ikorodu connector. While the infrastructure project aims to expand multi-axle freight throughput, rainy-season pavement washouts have lengthened unmanaged shift worker commute times by an average of 42%, causing high-speed automated packaging lines across FMCG and food processing facilities to sit idle during morning changeovers.

► WHAT IT MEANS FOR PLANT OPERATORS

When machine operators arrive 35 minutes late due to highway detours, automated conveyor lines stand stationary while captive gas power plants and steam boilers burn expensive unrecovered fuel. Continuous processing plants cannot rely on informal Danfo transit during highway construction phases. Leading manufacturing operations deploy pre-mapped arterial bypasses with centralized IVMS telematics to redirect shift shuttles before they enter stationary bottlenecks.

Captive Gas Turbine Baseload Costs Rise Under Industrial Fuel Escalation

With industrial gas tariffs linked to commodity pricing benchmarks, running a 3.2 MW captive gas turbine without product load now costs over ₦750,000 per hour. Every minute an automated line stands idle waiting for machine operators directly inflates plant operating overhead.

Ogun TRACE Launches Morning Route Discipline Taskforce on Sango-Agbara Axis

Ogun State traffic authorities have stepped up vehicle roadworthiness impoundments targeting informal commercial buses along the Sango-Ota and Agbara industrial corridors, making audited, compliant corporate transport essential for shift reliability.

MAN Study: Managed Route Clustering Achieves 99.2% Punctuality in Heavy Plants
