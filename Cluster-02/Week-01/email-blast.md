### Cover Story: Energy Logistics & Safety Compliance
**HEADLINE: Upstream Operators Tighten Journey Management Audits Across Port Harcourt & Lekki Corridors Following Heavy Corridor Regulatory Crackdown**

*Photo: Heavy transport vehicles and oilfield crew shuttles undergoing pre-clearance inspections at the Onne Free Zone access terminal. Source: Upstream Industry News / Energy Logistics Nigeria.*

International Oil Companies (IOCs) and marginal field operators across the Niger Delta and Lagos offshore basins have initiated rigorous compliance crackdowns on third-party contractor personnel logistics. In a joint directive issued by major asset operators in Port Harcourt and the Lekki Free Trade Zone, safety auditors have been instructed to enforce zero-tolerance gate inspections on all contractor crew vehicles mobilizing to supply bases, flow stations, and export terminals.

According to the Oil & Gas Contractors Safety Association (OGCSA), over 34% of commercial charter buses deployed by secondary contractors over the past quarter failed basic roadworthiness audits, citing non-functional speed governors, missing IVMS data feeds, and inadequate passenger restraint systems. Contractors whose vehicles fail security gates face mandatory stop-work notices and financial liability for deferred marine crew departures.

> **► WHAT IT MEANS FOR HSE DIRECTORS & OPERATIONS MANAGERS:**
> Engaging informal, unvetted roadside bus charters is now an existential contractual risk. When an IOC auditor red-cards a personnel bus at the gate, the delay does not merely inconvenience workers—it strands offshore vessels, delays helicopter turnarounds, and incurs tens of millions of naira in standby day rates. Energy contractors must mandate audited, IVMS-compliant corporate transport partners capable of providing verifiable electronic pre-trip inspection records.
